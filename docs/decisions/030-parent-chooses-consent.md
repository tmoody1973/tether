# 030 — The parent chooses how consent works; refunds come back; the total includes tax and shipping

**Decision** — Amara chooses between standing consent ("up to $X per purchase without asking me") and asking before every purchase. She can pause spending at once and turn investing off. A refund gives the money back, either to Zoe's week or to what Amara has promised. Every total Zoe sees, and every charge, includes Tether's estimates of sales tax and shipping, with the product's photo and its condition when it isn't new.

**Why this came up** — Parent feedback relayed by Tarik on 8 Nov 2026. The level decided who approves what, and parents wanted to choose for themselves. Some want a standing "fine up to $15", others want to see every purchase. Their only emergency control was Stop, which also disconnects the saved PayPal. A refunded purchase stayed on Zoe's record as spent. Investing came with Level 2 whether the parent wanted it or not. And Zoe saw item prices only, so "fits your week" could be wrong by the cost of tax and shipping. Getting this wrong means a parent who doesn't trust the defaults, or a teen whose numbers don't match what leaves the parent's PayPal.

**Options**
1. **Keep approval tied to the level; add Pause only.** Cost: parents who want "every purchase" or a different limit can't have it. The level stays a bundle they didn't choose.
2. **Amara's choices become the rules she signs (chosen).** The spending mode, the investing switch and the cost estimates are stored in the signed rules, so each change is a new version that Proof can show. Pause is a separate switch on the family, so it's instant and reversible without re-signing. Cost: more settings on the rules page, and every reader of the rules has to respect them.
3. **Let the AI suggest a mode from her history.** Cost: the consent surface must never be decided by a model (the rules page says so), and parents asked to choose themselves.

**What we chose and why** — Tarik approved the brief (spending mode, pause, investing switch, refunds, estimates in the total, photos and condition). Claude made the engine calls:
- **Spending mode turns into the same approval rules the engine already checks.** "Every purchase" becomes one rule that always asks. "Up to $X" replaces the level's amount, and blank means no per-purchase limit (Level 3's default). Level 1's "a shop she hasn't used" check stays unless she picks "every purchase". Over the amount it asks Amara; it isn't refused. The amount must be whole cents from $1 to the weekly amount. Her choice carries across every level change, Amara's own and the automatic level-up, and is never loosened. "Every purchase" and "no limit" stay as they are; an "up to" amount stays, capped at the new level's weekly money. Only Amara replaces it. The rules page previews the new level's rules with that same carry rule, so the clause she confirms is the one that's signed, and the level confirm says so ("Your spending choice stays: over $5.00 comes to you first"). (Review HIGH 1: an earlier version dropped a custom amount on any level change, so a $5 limit could silently become Level 2's $20.)
- **Pause works inside the engine, not as a screen-only block.** While paused, every purchase gets an extra approval reason. Zoe can still find things and ask. Buy can't go through: the pause is re-checked at payment, under the same lock as the budget, so pausing between Buy and payment stops it. Amara's yes clears it, because approving is her deliberate act. The shopping helper is switched off while paused, as the brief asked. Rules, the saved PayPal, the week and the savings promise are untouched. The old teen state that meant "Amara stopped your rules" is renamed "stopped", so "paused" only ever means a pause.
- **Investing off** is a flag in the signed rules. The engine refuses new proposals with it, and also at the moment an already-approved investment would be placed. Nothing already invested is sold, and Zoe can still see her investments.
- **Refunds** are found where PayPal shows them: reconciliation with PayPal's transaction list. There is no refund webhook in Tether today. A purchase's refund goes back into its week if that week hasn't closed, so she can spend it. If the week has closed, it is added to what Amara has promised. When the week has closed, only the share that came out of her allowance is added, using week close's own formula. A refund of money Amara approved past the week adds nothing, so the result is the same whether the refund was seen before or after the week closed (review MEDIUM 2). Tether writes only the difference between PayPal's refunded total for that payment and what it has already written, keyed so a replay or a second check writes nothing (a database index enforces it). Partial refunds add up, never past what was charged. A purchase that PayPal declined, and Tether already reversed, is never refunded again.
- **Late refunds are still found, within limits.** A month's check normally searches PayPal for 31 days from the month's first purchase. For that month's payments only, it also searches the time after that window, in 31-day steps, up to 180 days after the window started (PayPal's usual refund limit) and never past today. That's at most five more searches, and none for the current month. If one of those searches fails, it is skipped and tried again at the next check (review MEDIUM 3). A refund more than about 180 days after the purchase isn't looked for.
- **Tax and shipping are estimates, because nothing better exists.** Channel3's docs (read 8 Nov) list Checkout as "coming soon" with no endpoint. An offer carries price, availability, condition and dimensions, but no tax and no shipping. So Amara enters her sales tax (required when she signs new rules) and a shipping estimate per order ($5.99 by default). Tether adds tax, rounded half up on the item total, and one shipping charge per order. The total is what the budget, the approval rules, the permission slip, the request page and the PayPal charge all use. PayPal is sent the split (items, tax, shipping), built from whole cents, and the charge isn't sent if the parts don't add up exactly. The words say plainly these are estimates. In a real launch the store's checkout gives the actual total, and if it's higher than what was approved, Tether asks again.
- **Condition: Channel3's SDK (4.8) states "new", "used" or nothing.** It treats "refurbished" as deprecated and returns it as nothing, so Tether can say "Used" or "Condition not stated", but never "Refurbished". Search has a condition filter, and it defaults to new items, plus items whose condition isn't stated. Tether keeps that default and labels anything that isn't new, rather than filtering more. A pasted link can still bring a used item, and it says so. The condition is part of the cart, and payment re-checks it, so an offer that changed from new to used since Zoe saw it is refused.
- **Photos** come from Channel3's product images: the main one, and Channel3's cleaned version when there is one. Only https links are used. Photos are shown at a fixed size, loaded lazily, and don't tell the image host which page they're on. Offer links stay hidden, because they're affiliate links.
- **Demo history stays as it was charged.** The shared demo family's past purchases were made with no estimates. The seed switches the demo's estimates on afterwards (8% tax, $5.99 shipping, labelled "Demo value"), so only new carts include them. A demo reset clears a judge's pause. A demo snapshot taken before this change has no estimates; the next reset (including the automatic one after 6 hours) gives it the demo's and saves that back once, so the live demo shows tax and shipping without a production re-seed. A judge who stopped the demo's rules can sign new ones without typing a tax: the demo values are used (review MEDIUM 4).

- **Deploy order: migration 0020 must run on production before this code is deployed.** Every page that loads a family asks the database for the new `paused_at` column, so code deployed without it is a full outage, not just broken purchases (review MEDIUM 5). The migration only adds things, so running it first is safe with the old code still live.

**What we gave up**
- Totals are estimates. A real order could cost a little more or less, and Tether can't confirm the difference without a store checkout.
- Pause blocks the shopping helper outright, so while paused Zoe finds things by search or by pasting a link.
- Refunds only appear when Amara's Activity page (or the assistant's PayPal check) asks PayPal, and PayPal's list can lag a few hours. With no refund webhook, nothing applies a refund in the background.
- The per-purchase limits are compared with the estimated total, not the item price. With $5.99 shipping, Level 1's "anything over $10" now asks Amara for any item over about $3.70, so a Level 1 teen asks much more often.
- Looking for late refunds costs up to five more PayPal searches when Amara opens an earlier month's Activity.
- Rules signed before this change carry no estimates until Amara sets them. Their carts are checked on the item price, as before.
- With refunds to apply, the Activity page's PayPal check takes a little longer. That check already redraws the purchase list when it finishes, so a Proof opened before then closes once.

**How we'll know if this was right** — In the sandbox: switching to "every purchase" makes a $1 cart ask Amara. Pausing shows "Paused by Amara" on Zoe's screens, and resuming brings Buy back. A $12.99 item at 8% tax with $5.99 shipping shows $20.02 everywhere and charges $20.02 to PayPal, with a breakdown of 12.99 + 1.04 + 5.99. A refund in PayPal shows in Proof as "Refunded in PayPal · $X back to Zoe's week" and changes what she has left, once. Parents stop asking "can I approve every one?" and "why didn't the refund show?".
