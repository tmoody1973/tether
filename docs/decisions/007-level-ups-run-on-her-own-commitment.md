# 007 — Level-ups run on the teen's own savings commitment

**Decision** — The teen moves up a level by keeping a weekly savings amount *she* chose (at least 10% of her allowance) for four weeks running.

**Why this came up** — Once savings became "the part of the allowance she didn't spend" (decision 003), the old promotion rule ("spend no more than 90% and save at least 30%") was really one condition written twice. And the rule was the parent's number, not the teen's.

**Options**
1. **A fixed 30% savings target.** Cost: the parent's goal, not hers; nothing for her to own.
2. **Her own commitment, with a floor the parent sets.** Cost: a teen could pick the minimum every time; promotion becomes slower to tune.
3. **No savings condition, only "stayed in bounds."** Cost: under the new money model she can't go out of bounds (the engine blocks it), so everyone would be promoted.

**What we chose and why** — Option 2. Claude recommended it; Tarik approved. It was already in the PRD (FR-7.2, the teen writes her own commitment). People who set their own savings goal and lock it save more (Ashraf, Karlan & Yin 2006, adults). And it powers the "Headphones: 3 weeks away" sentences (decision 005). Guardrails: the parent sets a 10% floor; the amount changes only at week start; a missed week pauses progress and never demotes; only the parent can demote, with a recorded reason.

**What we gave up** — A single universal rule that's easy to explain on one slide. Evidence for 13–17-year-olds specifically is thin.

**How we'll know if this was right** — In use, most teens set a commitment above the floor, and fewer than half of missed weeks are followed by a second miss.
