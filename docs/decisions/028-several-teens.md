# 028 — A parent with several teens: name them all at the start, switch with one tap, see what's waiting on each

**Decision** — Amara can have more than one teen on Tether, from her first visit. The teen form takes up to five teens at once. A teen switcher sits at the top of every parent page: the current teen's initial and name, and a list of her teens, each with what's waiting for her there, plus "Add a teen". Each teen is a separate family behind the scenes, with its own rules, budget, record, investing account, teen link and saved PayPal. Settings is the full list. The menu becomes Home, {Teen}'s rules, Savings, Investing, Activity, Settings. On a phone the bottom bar keeps the first five, and Settings becomes a gear beside the switcher. A new Savings page explains what "savings" means on Tether.

**Why this came up** — A parent with two or three teens couldn't add the second one. Tether already stored "one parent, several families" (decision 026: one row per parent-and-teen pair, all sharing her PayPal id), but nothing on screen let her make the second family, and once signed in she couldn't move between teens: her sign-in cookie names one family only. The "Which teen?" page existed but in practice nobody reached it. Getting this wrong on a money app means one teen's rules or money showing up under another teen's name, or one person's browser opening someone else's family.

**Options**
1. **One family, several teens inside it (rejected).** Rework the data so a family holds many teens. Cost: every rule, budget, record, investing and payment query assumes one teen per family; days of rework four days before the deadline, with a real chance of mixing two teens' money.
2. **One family per teen, a switcher on top (chosen).** Keep the data model. Add "Add another teen" (a fresh PayPal login, then the same teen form as starting), remember who PayPal said she is in a sealed cookie (signed by the server so it can't be edited, holding only her PayPal id), and a Settings page that lists and switches. Cost: she saves her PayPal once per teen, and each teen's family is retired on its own 7-day clock.
3. **Several teens, one shared saved PayPal.** Like option 2, but copy the first teen's saved PayPal to the second. Cost: retiring one family deletes its saved PayPal at PayPal, which would silently break the sibling's payments. Rejected for that reason.

**What we chose and why** — Option 2. Tarik asked for it on 8 Nov 2026, then asked for several teens at onboarding, an always-visible switcher, a Savings page and its explainer; Claude made the design calls. The details that matter:

- *Several teens at the start.* The teen form has "+ Add another teen", up to five rows, each name and age checked as for one teen, no name twice. One PayPal login makes one family per teen. The one-time ticket from that login is spent once for the whole batch, so replaying it makes nothing. Each teen counts against the daily cap of 30 new families. The cap is taken for the whole list at once: if it can't take them all, none is made and the page says so ("Today's new families can't fit all your teens, so none were made. Nothing changed."). We chose "none" over "the ones that fit" because a parent who named three teens and got two would have to work out which one is missing; with none, she can just try again. She lands on the first teen's Home and its own three-step checklist.
- *The teen switcher.* It shows whenever she's signed in through PayPal (for the shared judge login: whenever the current teen is one this browser made), even with one teen, so adding a teen is easy to find. It's a button that opens a list (a "disclosure": the button says whether the list is open; Escape or a tap outside closes it and Escape puts focus back on the button). Each teen has a stable colour from its id. Picking a teen posts to the same ownership-checked switch route as Settings, carrying the tab she's on, so Maya's rules open Zoe's rules. Only a fixed list of tabs is accepted, so the form can't send her anywhere else.
- *Nothing missed.* Each teen in the switcher shows the same count Home's tab shows (the first-run steps still open, or the "Needs you" items), and the switcher button adds up the other teens' counts, so a request from Maya shows while she's on Zoe. Counts are worked out only for teens she's allowed to switch to, at most 10, by running Home's own summary once per teen. That's a few database reads per teen on every page; one counting query is the upgrade if parents ever have more than a handful of teens.
- *Notifications follow the parent, not the teen.* Before, each family had its own push address (an "alias": an opaque id OneSignal, the push service, delivers to). A phone that turned notifications on while she was on Zoe heard only Zoe. Now a parent who signed in with her own PayPal gets one alias for all her teens (derived from her PayPal id), so that phone hears Maya too; every push already starts with the teen's name ("Maya is asking for $12.00 at Target"). The shared judge login, the demo family and any doubtful case keep one alias per family, because judges share one PayPal id and must never get each other's pushes. A parent who turned notifications on before this change is moved to the new alias the next time she opens Settings with notifications allowed.
- *Savings gets its own menu item, and never reads as money held.* Savings and Investing look alike but aren't. Savings is a promise on the record: Tether counts what the teen didn't spend as an amount the parent promises, holds nothing and sets nothing aside, and the parent pays it herself and taps "Record payment to {teen}". Investing actually moves money into the teen's own account. So the figure is labelled "Promised to {teen}", with the line "This is your recorded commitment, not a separate PayPal balance. Tether has not reserved or transferred this money."; the teen form says up front that Tether doesn't hold money or create a savings account; and the Savings page opens with "Tether tracks allowance and savings promises. It doesn't hold or set aside money." It keeps three plain what-ifs (paying the teen, a low PayPal balance, investing). What happens when the parent changes the amount or stops is being changed on another branch, so the page links to the rules page for that instead of describing it here. A test pins every one of these sentences. The page and Settings also link to the parent's statement (/g/statement), which another branch adds.

- *"Add another teen" re-proves it's her.* The button goes to Log in with PayPal with an "add" flag. That flag is sealed into the same signed cookie that protects the login round trip (the "state", a random value that proves the browser coming back from PayPal is the one that left). The return page reads the flag only from that sealed cookie, never from the address bar, so pasting `?add=1` onto a link changes nothing.
- *Switching re-checks everything on the server.* The switch buttons post the family's id; the server signs her in to it only if the PayPal she last logged in with owns both the teen she's on now and the one she picked, and both are live. Signed in through a private guardian link or the shared demo family, Settings shows only the current teen and says "Log in with PayPal to see or add your other teens."
- *The shared judge login stays per browser.* Judges all use one PayPal sandbox account (decision 026). For that login, the list Settings shows and the switch route accepts comes from the browser's own cookie: the families THIS browser made, at most the last 10, and only those that are still live and still owned by that PayPal. Never another judge's family, never a list built from the PayPal id alone. Logging back in with the shared login opens the newest family this browser made.
- *The one-per-browser-per-hour limit doesn't apply to adding a teen.* Starting a family sets a one-hour cookie that refuses a second start from the same browser. That would stop a real parent adding two teens back to back, so a teen added through "Add another teen" from an ordinary PayPal account skips it (that account has its own cap, below); the shared judge login doesn't. The site-wide cap of 30 new families a day still applies to every add; when it's used up she sees "Today's new families are used up, so no teen was added. Nothing changed." and stays signed in to the teen she was on.
- *Settings-type cards moved off Home.* The notifications card and Reset demo moved from Home to Settings, and the teen's link (send and reset) moved there from the rules page, where it had gone only because there was no Settings page. Home keeps the first-run checklist, which still links to the send-link page, and its demo clock.
- *The fix that came with it.* Links styled as filled buttons (Home's "Save your PayPal" step among them) showed dark text on green because a general link rule outranked the button style. One CSS rule fixes all of them.

**Fixed after the independent review (8 Nov 2026)**
- *A page left open on one teen can't change another.* Switching teens in one browser tab changes the sign-in for every tab. So every parent form now says which teen it was drawn for: signing rules, changing the level, Stop, shops, the link step, Reset demo and the demo clock. So does every "Save your PayPal" link (carried through the PayPal round trip in its cookie). If that isn't the teen she's signed in to now, nothing happens and Home says "That page was for a teen you've since switched away from, so nothing changed." Without this, a Stop left open on Liam's page could have stopped Maya's rules and deleted Maya's saved PayPal.
- *A push for one teen opens that teen's request.* Pushes now reach her phone for all her teens, so a tap can open Maya's request while she's on Zoe. The request page looks among the teens she may switch to, says "This is Maya's request.", and offers "Switch to Maya", which lands on that request. A request from a family she can't open still shows the calm "This request has closed" page, which never says whose it is.
- *The shared judge account is recognised by its PayPal id for good.* Anyone with the published password can change that account's email. Tether then recognised it only through families stored under the shared email, and those lost their PayPal id when they were retired after 7 days. Now a retired family made with the shared account keeps that account's PayPal id and email; they are published test details, not anyone's personal data. Every other retired family's PayPal identity is wiped as before. Tarik will fill in the shared account's PayPal payer id (`SANDBOX_LOGIN.payerId` in src/ui/sandboxAccount.ts) before judging, which adds a second check that no email change can defeat.
- *A batch of teens is one database transaction*: if anything fails partway, none is made and the day's count doesn't move.
- *One PayPal account can add at most 5 new teens in any 24 hours*, on top of the site's 30 a day. The shared judge login can't have that cap, because judges share one PayPal id; instead it keeps the one-family-per-browser-per-hour limit even for "Add another teen", so one browser can't use up the site's day. (A judge can still name several teens on one form.) The count is checked before the one-time ticket is spent, so she can send the form again with fewer teens; two forms sent at the same instant could both pass.
- *The month assistant checks the teen too.* A question asked from a page drawn for a teen she has since switched away from gets "You switched teens in another tab; nothing was asked.", with no AI call and nothing taken from the day's allowance, so an answer about one teen never appears on the other's page.
- *Names must differ from her existing teens*, any case. On the shared login the comparison is with this browser's teens only.
- *Setup steps aren't "waiting".* The switcher shows a teen's unfinished setup as "3 setup steps left" in plain grey. Only asks and messages (and, after setup, Home's "Needs you" items) get the amber "waiting" count.
- *A device that had notifications on keeps working.* Home quietly re-attaches a device that already allowed notifications to the parent's push address, as the card did when it lived on Home. It never prompts.
- *The teen form keeps what she typed.* If the server sends the form back with a problem, the names and ages come back from this browser tab's own storage.

**What we gave up**
- A batch is all or nothing against the daily cap: on a nearly full day, a parent naming three teens gets none, where two would have fitted.
- Every parent page now does a little more work: one Home summary per teen she can switch to, to show the counts.
- Notifications for the shared judge login still follow one teen: a judge's phone hears only the teen it was on when notifications were turned on.
- She saves her PayPal once per teen. One PayPal approval per teen is a minute each, but it is repetition a real product would remove.
- The hourly per-browser limit is weaker for ordinary PayPal accounts: they can make several families an hour through "Add another teen", up to their 5 a day. On the shared judge login the hourly limit still holds, but it was always easy to get round by clearing cookies; the daily site cap of 30 is the limit that actually holds.
- On the shared judge login, a teen added in one browser can't be reached from another browser, and the list remembers only the last 10 families per browser.
- Settings knows she logged in with PayPal only for 7 days after her last PayPal login (the life of the remembered-PayPal cookie). After that, Settings shows one teen until she logs in with PayPal again.
- The teen's link no longer appears on the rules page; a parent used to it there has to find it in Settings.

**How we'll know if this was right** — On the deployed sandbox: a parent who names two teens on the first form, then switches back and forth, sees each teen's own name on that teen's rules page, with no teen's rules, money or link ever shown under the other's name; an ask from one teen shows on the switcher while she's on the other. A parent with her own PayPal who turned notifications on while on one teen gets a push naming the other teen when that teen asks. Two judges on the shared login in two browsers each see only their own teens in Settings. The usage_pool table never shows more than 30 family starts in a day.
