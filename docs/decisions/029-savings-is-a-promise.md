# 029 — Savings is a promise: weekly changes wait for next week, stopping keeps this week, and a closing statement says what is owed

**Decision** — Tether calls savings what it is, an amount the parent has promised, and treats it that way: a change to the weekly amount starts next week unless the parent chooses this week too after seeing exactly what that means; stopping the rules no longer wipes out the week it happens in; and a printable closing statement shows what has been recorded, paid and is still owed.

**Why this came up** — Parent feedback relayed by Tarik on 8 Nov 2026. Three things read as unfair or untrue. First, lowering the allowance mid-week cut the current week at once, so a teen who had planned around $30 could find herself "over" a $20 week she never agreed to. Second, "Stop Zoe's rules" quietly zeroed the current week, so whatever she hadn't spent that week stopped counting as saved. Third, the words said savings "stays in your PayPal", which suggests money set aside somewhere; Tether never holds or reserves money (decision 001), so savings is only a figure the parent has promised to honour. Getting this wrong costs trust with exactly the people the product is for: a parent who feels tricked by the wording, or a teen who feels the rules changed under her.

**Options**
1. **Keep the current behaviour and only fix the words.** Cost: the wording would become honest while the behaviour stayed surprising: a mid-week cut and a stop still take money off a week already under way.
2. **Store a scheduled weekly amount on the family and switch it over when the week closes** (the way Zoe's own savings promise already waits, `pendingCommitmentCents`). Cost: the week only "closes" when the demo clock moves (or the seed runs); nothing closes weeks on a real Monday. Until something did, the purchase check would keep using the old amount into the new week, and the check, Zoe's "left this week" and the week close could disagree.
3. **Keep the change on the new rules themselves, with a note that says "until next Monday, this week's amount is still $X"** (chosen). Every place that asks "how much is this week?" reads the rules through one small function that applies the note while it lasts. Cost: one more field on the signed rules, and every reader of the weekly amount must go through that function (a reader that forgets would show next week's amount early).

**What we chose and why** — Tarik asked for these behaviours (next-week default, an explicit "Change this week too" with its consequence shown first, stop keeps this week, a closing statement, and the honest words). Claude made the engine calls:
- **Option 3 for scheduling.** A level change installs a new rules version straight away, so shop removals, stricter approvals and a lower level's limits apply at once and receipts still name the exact version that allowed them. If the weekly amount differs from the one covering this week, the new version carries `thisWeek` (the old amount and next Monday's date). The purchase check, Zoe's and Amara's "left this week", the request card, the month assistant and the week close all read it through `asOf`, so they cannot disagree. The note is clock-based, so it expires on Monday with no job needed. Past weeks keep the allowance they were closed with (closed weeks are stored and never recomputed).
- **Several changes in one week keep the week's original amount** (Level 1, then 2 on Tuesday, then 3 on Wednesday: this week stays $20). A savings change re-signs the rules but keeps the note too.
- **"Change this week too"** applies the new amount now. The confirm box shows "This week becomes $X. Zoe has spent $Y, so she has $Z left, and this week adds $Z to what you've promised instead of $W." If she has already spent more than the new amount, it says by how much. If what's left falls below her savings promise, it adds: "so this week won't count toward her next level." The numbers come from the same "unspent" calculation and the same promise the week close uses.
- **The confirm only applies to the numbers Amara saw.** The page sends this week's amount and what Zoe had spent when the box was drawn. The server re-reads both while holding the family lock. If either changed (say Zoe bought something in between), nothing changes, and the page shows the new consequence for her to confirm again.
- **A level-up the system grants automatically after four kept weeks still applies at once.** It isn't the parent re-signing, it's a reward, and it happens at a month boundary.
- **Stopping records the time.** A new `revoked_at` column (a column is one field on every row of a database table) says when the rules were stopped. The week the stop falls in closes with the allowance that was in force, so what she didn't spend is still recorded as promised. Weeks after the stop give nothing, as before. This replaces the old shortcut where a stopped rule gave $0 for every week not yet closed.
- **A week with a stop in it never counts toward a level-up.** A new `stopped` flag on the closed week makes it "not kept", even if the parent re-signs later that same week. So it breaks a run of kept weeks the way a missed week does. Reason: a level-up is evidence of steady saving under rules in force, and a week the parent had to stop is not that evidence.
- **The statement adds up from the ledger, never a second formula.** "Recorded so far" is the savings figure Zoe and Amara already see, built from the same ledger rows and signs. The payments it lists come from those same rows, so a payment a demo reset set aside is in neither the list nor the total. The headline, "You owe Zoe", is that figure plus what this week adds when it closes (zero when no rules cover the week). After a stop nothing more can be spent, so the headline is final, and once the week closes it equals the figure Zoe sees. While rules are in force it is labelled "counting this week so far".
- **On the shared demo family, a stop belongs to the judge who made it.** A demo reset clears stop times from after the seed, just as it already removes the weeks they closed. So one judge's Stop never marks the next judge's week as stopped or broken.
- **After Stop, Amara lands on the statement.** The page shows PayPal's outcome on the saved wallet, this week counted, and a Print button.

**What we gave up**
- A weekly cut no longer protects the parent's money this week by default. A parent who wants it now must take the extra step, and sees the cost first.
- The `thisWeek` note is a rule every reader must follow. Missing it in a new screen would show next week's amount early.
- While rules are in force, the statement's headline can still move as Zoe spends. It's only final once rules are stopped or the week closes.
- If Zoe spends while Amara is reading the "Change this week too" box, Amara has to confirm a second time on the new numbers.
- Signed rules from before this change keep their old "stays in your PayPal" sentence. They are stored as signed and are not rewritten.
- On the shared demo family the stop confirmation can't say "disconnects your saved PayPal" (it keeps the PayPal so the next judge can try it, decision C1). It says so instead of using the exact sentence.

**How we'll know if this was right** — In the sandbox: move a teen from Level 1 to 2 on a Wednesday and the rules page says "From Mon …: $30.00/week. This week stays $20.00.", a $9 cart with $8 left is refused, and the closed week reads $20. Stop the rules mid-week and the statement says "Rules stopped on …. This week still counts as promised.", then after the week closes the savings figure has grown by exactly the amount it said. Parents who tried the earlier version no longer describe the mid-week cut or the stop as unfair.
