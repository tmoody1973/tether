# 025 — Stocks sit alongside funds, with the same rules

**Decision** — Zoe can propose a single company's stock, not just a fund, under exactly the same rules: Level 2 and up, her own answers to three questions, and Amara's yes on every one. Amara's "fund list" becomes her "investing list". A stock's page says what the company is, from its SEC filings. Leveraged, inverse and daily-target products can't be proposed at all, and a share under $1 gets one plain line saying such prices can swing a lot.

**Why this came up** — Tarik, 7 November: "Why are we just using funds, we should do stocks too." The search already found stocks, and a stock could already be proposed (checked live: Zoe at Level 2 found Apple, saw its price and its one-year and five-year lines, and could ask Amara to add it). But every word on screen still said "fund", a stock's page had nothing about what the company does, and nothing stopped a product built to move three times as fast as the market for one day. Getting this wrong means a parent approving something she can't see inside, or a teen holding a product designed for day traders.

**Options**
1. **Funds only.** Hide stocks from the search. Cost: the search stops being "the whole market", Zoe can't follow a company she actually cares about, and the app would be deciding for the family what counts as a sensible investment, which Tether promises not to do.
2. **Stocks too, same rules (chosen).** Nothing new to approve or learn: a stock goes on Amara's list the same way a fund does. Add "About the company" from Financial Datasets' company facts (name, sector, industry, where it's based), block leveraged and inverse products by their names, and add the penny line. Cost: one more paid data lookup per stock (capped per family per day and cached for a week), and a name-based block that misses an inverse fund whose name doesn't say so.
3. **Stocks with stricter rules.** For example a lower cap or a second confirmation for a single stock. Cost: two sets of rules for Amara to understand, and Tether starting to rank one kind of investment above another, which edges toward advice.

**What we chose and why** — Option 2, Tarik's call. It keeps one set of rules and leaves the judgement with Amara, while giving her what she needs to make it: on the request she sees "Apple: Technology · Consumer Electronics · Cupertino, CA" before she taps. The leveraged block is the one place Tether says no on its own. Because they reset every day, held for months they can lose money even when the market ends up higher, the opposite of how Zoe is meant to invest (for years), so offering them would undercut everything else. The block runs in three places: the search shows "Not available on Tether", the server refuses a proposal even if the form is posted directly, and Amara's yes is refused for any proposal made before the rule existed.

**What we gave up** — The simpler safety story of "only broad funds, nothing else". A single stock can fall much further than the whole market, and Tether now lets a teen ask for one; Amara's approval is the only thing between them. Each stock page also spends one more paid lookup the first time a family opens it each week. And the name check is a heuristic, a rule of thumb that reads the product's name: "ProShares Short S&P500" is an inverse fund with no telling word in its name, and it passes.

**How we'll know if this was right** — In the demo a visitor can propose a stock, and Amara's request shows what the company does before she approves. Searching for "Apple" shows the leveraged Apple product as "Not available on Tether". If visitors ask why a particular fund was blocked or let through, that's the signal the name check needs a better source, such as an issuer's own flag.
