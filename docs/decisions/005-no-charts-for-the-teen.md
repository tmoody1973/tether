# 005 — No charts on the teen's screens; teach at the moment of decision

**Decision** — The teen sees short sentences at the moments she decides, not spending charts or lessons.

**Why this came up** — Tarik asked whether charts would help teach a teenager about money. The design rules already banned charts on the teen side, but on taste, not evidence. Getting it wrong either clutters the product or misses the impact story judges score.

**Options**
1. **Charts on the teen side** (spending by week, by category). Cost: research shows education mostly raises *knowledge*, not *behaviour*; charts invite comparison, and people stop looking at money dashboards when the news is bad.
2. **Lessons or quizzes** (what Greenlight, GoHenry and BusyKid sell). Cost: puts Tether in their category, which hurts the Innovation score; and classroom-style money lessons fade within about 20 months.
3. **Sentences at the moment of decision.** Cost: less visually impressive in a demo; relies on good copy.

**What we chose and why** — Option 3. Claude's call, based on the research: the largest review of financial education found it explains about 0.1% of behaviour and recommends "just in time" teaching tied to a specific decision (Fernandes, Lynch & Netemeyer, 2014). Tether's refusal-with-arithmetic already does this. Three additions close the gaps: a line on approved purchases ("sets Headphones back 1 week"), two or three reflection sentences when the week ends, and weeks-to-goal as the goal screen's one number.

**What we gave up** — A chart-heavy "insights" screen that looks impressive in a video.

**How we'll know if this was right** — In testing, a teen can say how many weeks away her goal is without opening anything; judges remember the line "no lessons — the learning happens at the moment she decides."
